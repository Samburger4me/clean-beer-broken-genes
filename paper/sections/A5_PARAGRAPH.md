## Lager assemblies carry no *S. eubayanus* copy of the pathway

Lager yeast, *S. pastorianus*, is a hybrid of *S. cerevisiae* and the cold-tolerant *S. eubayanus* [@libkind2011; @dunn2008; @baker2015], and wild *S. eubayanus* parents bring POF back into new lager hybrids [@mertens2019]. A lager strain could therefore keep clove flavor through its *S. eubayanus* genes even when its *S. cerevisiae* copy is broken. We located *PAD1* and *FDC1* in the *S. eubayanus* reference genome ({{R['a5_se_reference_assembly']}}; {{f0(R['a5_sc_vs_se_FDC1_aa_identity_pct'])}}% amino-acid identity to *S. cerevisiae* Fdc1) and searched both complete lager assemblies with sensitive blastn, using the *MAL/MPH* genes as positive controls (found in {{R['a5_blast_batches_with_control_hit']}} search batches). Neither assembly contains an *S. eubayanus* copy of either gene (Fig. 6c). CBS 1483 has one *S. cerevisiae* copy of each: an intact *PAD1* and an *FDC1* with the same c.501dupA frameshift found in the lager record of Mukai *et al.* [@mukai2014]. CBS 1513 has no copy of either gene from either parent. In the *S. eubayanus* reference the pair lies only {{f1(R['a5_se_ref_cluster_to_chr_end_nt']/1000)}} kb from a chromosome end, and the CBS 1513 record that would carry the *S. cerevisiae* locus is a recombinant chromosome (ScIV–SeII, CP150027.1), consistent with loss of these ends by rearrangement. These two assemblies therefore suggest that lager yeasts are POF− because the *S. eubayanus* genes are missing and the *S. cerevisiae* *FDC1* is broken, not because of the frameshift alone. With only two genomes, and with no read-level check that the missing genes are not simply unassembled, we treat this as a hypothesis.
